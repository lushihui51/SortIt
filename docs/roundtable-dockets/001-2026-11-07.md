## Product decisions

- PD1 The app is one screen holding a dimension picker, a pool in which every item of the set appears as a card, and, once a dimension is chosen, one zone per category of that dimension, each labelled with the category's name. The learner drags a card from the pool into any zone, from one zone to another, or from a zone back to the pool, in any order, until they have the board checked.
  - Facts: the learner sorts one fixed set of items into the categories of a dimension they pick; the app has one screen.
  - Rejected: one item at a time, because the learner cannot compare items or revisit a placement before the end. Cards dealt into zones at the start, because the learner cannot tell their own placements from the app's, and a card correct by chance teaches nothing.
  - Becomes true: the learner sees the whole set at once, places cards in any order, and can change any placement before checking.
- PD2 Before a dimension is chosen, the screen shows every card in the pool and the dimension picker, and no zone; no card can be placed until a dimension is chosen.
  - Facts: the set has more than one dimension, and picking one is the learner's first act.
  - Rejected: the first dimension chosen in advance, because the learner may never notice that other dimensions exist. An empty board until a dimension is chosen, because the learner learns nothing about the set before choosing.
  - Becomes true: the learner's first view shows the pile and the choice of how to sort it.
- PD3 The pool shows its cards in random order when the app loads and each time the board is cleared.
  - Facts: a learner who remembers positions can re-sort without recalling meanings.
  - Rejected: shuffling once per visit, because after a Reset the learner re-sorts by remembered position. The order of the data file, because the author's order can leak the grouping.
  - Becomes true: every fresh board is a fresh pile for the learner.
- PD4 Check enables only once the pool is empty, and judges every placement at once.
  - Facts: the quiz is about committing to a sort of the whole set.
  - Rejected: Check at any time with unplaced cards counted wrong, because the score then mixes "not placed" with "wrong". Judging each drop as it lands, because the sort becomes trial and error and nothing is committed.
  - Becomes true: the learner decides every card before seeing any verdict.
- PD5 After Check, each card shows whether it is correct or incorrect by a colour and an icon together, on the card where it lies.
  - Facts: some learners cannot tell the colours apart; the learner reads the verdict on the board.
  - Rejected: colour alone, because a colour-blind learner cannot read it. A results list apart from the board, because the learner has to map the list back to cards.
  - Becomes true: the learner reads the verdict of every card at a glance, with or without colour.
- PD6 An incorrect card shows only that it is incorrect, never the category it belongs to.
  - Facts: the learner can try again; each try narrows the choice.
  - Rejected: naming the right category on each wrong card, because the learner is told the answer at once and nothing is left to try.
  - Becomes true: the learner finds a wrong card's category by trying again.
- PD7 After Check, the score is shown beside the board as the count of correct cards over the count of all cards.
  - Facts: a set holds up to about 30 items, so a count is concrete.
  - Rejected: a percentage, because it is less concrete for a small set. No score, because the learner has to count marks.
  - Becomes true: the learner gets one number per check that says how many they got right out of how many.
- PD8 Reset returns every card to the pool and removes every mark and the score; the chosen dimension stays chosen; Reset acts at once, without a confirmation.
  - Facts: the common case for starting over is retrying the same dimension.
  - Rejected: Reset also unchoosing the dimension, because it adds a step to the common case. A confirmation before Reset, because it puts a dialog on every reset; the cost is that one mis-click can lose a full board.
  - Becomes true: the learner starts over on the same dimension with one action.
- PD9 Choosing another dimension clears the board as Reset does and shows the new dimension's zones; choosing the current dimension again does nothing.
  - Facts: the categories of one dimension differ from those of another, so a placement cannot carry across.
  - Rejected: a confirmation when cards are placed, because it puts a dialog on every change of dimension.
  - Becomes true: the learner moves between dimensions freely and always starts a dimension from a full pool.
- PD10 Retry is in the MVP. After a check, Retry returns only the incorrect cards to the pool, in random order, and removes their marks and the score; correct cards stay in their zones with their marks; Check enables again once the pool is empty.
  - Facts: no deadline is set that would cut it; the learner's next step after a verdict is to fix what was wrong.
  - Rejected: leaving Retry out, because the learner must redo every placement, correct ones included, to fix one mistake.
  - Becomes true: the learner works only on the cards they got wrong.
- PD11 Tap-to-select is in the MVP: a card can be selected by a tap or click and sent to a zone or back to the pool by a second tap or click on it, and the same can be done by keyboard alone.
  - Facts: the app serves a phone in portrait; no deadline is set that would cut it.
  - Rejected: dragging only, because a learner on a phone depends on touch dragging working and a keyboard user cannot play at all.
  - Becomes true: a learner on any served device, and one using only a keyboard, can sort the whole set.
- PD12 The app serves current desktop Chrome, Safari and Firefox, and a phone in portrait. A dimension of up to about 30 items and 6 categories fits a desktop screen without scrolling problems; beyond those sizes, and on a phone, the board still works and scrolls.
  - Facts: the first set is hand-written and small; the requirements name these devices and sizes.
  - Rejected: refusing a set beyond the sizes, because an author with 31 items meets an error instead of a crowded board. Desktop only, because a phone learner is turned away.
  - Becomes true: no learner is refused for their device and no set is refused for its size; only the fit without scrolling is promised, and only on desktop within the sizes.
- PD13 Every item has exactly one answer in every dimension, drawn from that dimension's categories. A set that breaks this shows, in place of the board, an error naming the item and the dimension at fault.
  - Facts: the set is edited by hand; the verdict and the score rest on one right answer per card.
  - Rejected: several correct categories for one item, because the verdict becomes "one of these" and the score loses meaning. A missing answer that skips the item for that dimension, because the learner sees different piles per dimension and cannot tell why. Skipping bad items silently, because a broken set looks like a smaller set.
  - Becomes true: the learner never meets a card with no right answer or two, and whoever edits the set is told what to fix.
- PD14 The app is named "Sort It".
  - Facts: the learner sees the name in the title; the set's subject may change.
  - Rejected: a name tied to the first set's subject, because it misnames every later set.
  - Becomes true: the learner meets a name that says what they do, whatever the subject.
- PD15 The MVP has no accounts, no saved progress, no leaderboards, no in-app editor for sets, one set only, no timers, no scoring history and no spaced repetition; a page refresh starts over with an empty board and no dimension chosen.
  - Facts: the MVP is kept short; the set lives in one hand-edited file.
  - Rejected: remembering the board in the browser across a refresh, because the learner can meet a stale board from an earlier day with no way to tell.
  - Becomes true: the learner gets a board to sort and nothing that outlives the visit.
- PD16 The first item set is a set of animals, written during the build and checked by the product owner. It exists to prove the app works and claims to teach nothing. It holds about 30 items and at least two dimensions, one of which has 6 categories, so the board is proved at the sizes PD12 promises.
  - Facts: the set only has to prove the app works; no subject or author for a teaching set is known.
  - Rejected: a handful of items, because a half-empty board proves nothing about the promised sizes. A real subject now, because it needs an author and facts nobody holds, and the set only has to prove the app.
  - Becomes true: whoever tests the app meets a full board, and every promise about sizes and set rules is exercised by the set that ships.
