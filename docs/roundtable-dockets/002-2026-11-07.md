## Code decisions

- CD1 The app shows Clerk's sign-in form only through Clerk's own modal, opened by `openSignIn` of `useClerk()`, for the "Sign in" button and for the form `SignedInOnly` opens alike, with Google's sign-in in a popup by `oauthFlow: "popup"`. The modal is never in Clerk's combined flow: it is given no `withSignUp`, and `ClerkProvider` is given no `signInUrl`.
  - Facts: LPD19 shows the form centred over the dimmed screen with the address unchanged through every step. ADR 008 mounts `<SignIn />` in a popover of the app's own with `routing="virtual"`, and ADR 017 has `SignedInOnly` open that popover and replace the address with `/` when the learner closes it. `@clerk/react` 6.18.8 loads `@clerk/ui` at major version 1 and `@clerk/clerk-js` at major version 6. In `@clerk/ui` 1.42.6, `Portal` gives `HashRouter` to a mounted component under every `routing` but `path`, `virtual` included, and `HashRouter` moves between steps by setting `window.location.hash`, so each step after the first changes the address and adds an entry to the browser's history; `RoutingOptions` accepts only `path` and `hash` for `SignIn`. `LazyModalRenderer` renders the modal that `openSignIn` opens under `VirtualRouter`, which keeps each step in React state and writes nothing to the address. `SignInModal` gives `signUpUrl` the virtual route `sign-up`, which `SignUpModal` renders in the same modal, unless the combined flow is on. The combined flow is on when the modal is given `withSignUp`, or when `ClerkProvider` is given a relative `signInUrl` and no `signUpUrl`, and it builds "Sign up" from `signInUrl`, which is Clerk's hosted page unless set. In the modal, the address Clerk goes to after signing in or making an account defaults to the current address, by `RedirectUrls` under mode `modal`. `SignInSocialButtons` opens Google's sign-in in a popup under `oauthFlow: "popup"`, in the modal as in a mounted form. The modal draws its own backdrop, locks the page's scroll and makes the page behind it inert. These facts come from reading Clerk's packages and are not confirmed by a run in a browser.
  - Rejected: ADR 008's `<SignIn routing="virtual" />` in a popover of the app's own, because it runs under `HashRouter` and each step after the first changes the address, against LPD19. `<SignIn routing="hash" />` in a dimmed modal of the app's own, for the same reason. `<SignInButton mode="modal">` around the app's button, because `SignedInOnly` opens the form without a press and still calls `openSignIn`, so one form has two ways of opening, each with props to keep in step. A form of the app's own on `useSignIn` and `useSignUp`, because every step Clerk can produce, its errors and email code checks included, becomes the app's to write and test. `openSignIn` with `withSignUp`, because "Sign up" then leaves the app for Clerk's hosted page, against PDR 013.
  - Becomes true: the next developer finds one call that opens the form, in `Account` in src/account/Account.tsx, and no popover, placement or z-index of the app's own for the form. A `withSignUp` or a `signInUrl` added later sends "Sign up" to Clerk's hosted page, and no test fails, since every test mocks `@clerk/react`.
- CD2 `Account` gives the modal, as `getContainer`, an element of its own that it renders into `document.body` through React's `createPortal`, and learns that the modal has closed when a `MutationObserver` on that element sees it emptied after holding the modal.
  - Facts: PDR 015 leaves a learner who closes the form that an address opened at the app's first address, so the app must know that the form closed. In `@clerk/ui` 1.42.6, `closeModal` sets Clerk's state for the modal to null and calls nothing of the app's, and `SignInModalProps` has no callback for a close. `getContainer` is a typed prop of `SignInModalProps`, which `LazyModalRenderer` hands to `UNSAFE_PortalProvider` and `Modal` takes as the root of its `FloatingPortal`, so the backdrop and the card render inside that element. Clerk renders the modal only while its state is set, so every close, by a press on the backdrop, Escape, the modal's cross or Clerk's navigation after signing in, removes it from that element. The backdrop is `position: fixed`, and an ancestor with a `transform`, a `filter` or a `contain` confines it. `__internal_addNavigationListener` fires only when Clerk navigates. These facts come from reading Clerk's packages and are not confirmed by a run in a browser.
  - Rejected: A `MutationObserver` on `document.body` for Clerk's `.cl-modalBackdrop`, because it rests on Clerk's class names where `getContainer` is a typed prop, and it watches every change of the page. The container rendered in place inside the bar, because a `transform` or a `contain` added later to the bar or above it confines the dimmed screen to the bar. `__internal_addNavigationListener`, because it is internal and never fires for a close by the backdrop or Escape. Looking for the modal on a timer, because it lags the close and runs for as long as the form is open.
  - Becomes true: `Account` alone knows how a close is observed, and names none of Clerk's markup. A Clerk that stops honouring `getContainer` renders the modal in `document.body`, the observer sees no close, a learner who closes the form at the list's or a deck's address stays at that address, and no test fails; the engineer's check in a browser is the one guard.
- CD3 `Account` keeps an observed close as state and calls `onSignInClose` from an effect only once `useAuth()` reports loaded and signed out while a close is held. A report of signed in drops the held close uncalled.
  - Facts: PDR 015 opens what the address names once the learner signs in, and brings them to the app's first address only if they close the form. ADR 017's `SignedInOnly` reads `useConvexAuth()` alone, and Convex confirms the learner's token after the modal has closed. In `@clerk/clerk-js` 6.43.1, `setActive` after a sign-in sets the session to `undefined` and emits, then navigates, and sets the new session and emits again only after `navigate` returns. `navigate` queues the navigation listeners, which close the modal, in a `setTimeout`, so the close lands while the session is `undefined` or just after it is set, by how long the app's `routerPush` takes. `resolveAuthState` in `@clerk/shared` gives `useAuth()` `isLoaded: false` while the session is `undefined`, and `isLoaded: true` with `isSignedIn: false` for a signed-out learner, whose session is `null`. A close by the backdrop, Escape or the cross leaves the session `null`. These facts come from reading Clerk's packages and are not confirmed by a run in a browser.
  - Rejected: Reading the Clerk object's `session` when the observer fires, because it is right only when the close lands after the session is set, and a miss sends a learner who has just signed in at the list's address to `/`. `Account` reporting every close with `SignedInOnly` deciding from `useConvexAuth()`, because the guard sees the learner not authenticated at the close after a sign-in and has to read Clerk's states to wait, against ADR 017. `stayOnPage` marking a sign-in when Clerk calls it, because it ties src/App.tsx to `Account` through shared state and takes any navigation of Clerk's for a sign-in. A fixed wait after the close, because a slow token outlasts it.
  - Becomes true: `SignedInOnly` takes `onSignInClose` to mean that the learner closed the form without signing in, and reads nothing of Clerk. The held close and the modal's container outlast the moment `useAuth()` reports not loaded, when `Account` shows nothing, and a test steps the mock of `useAuth` through signed out, not loaded and signed in.
- CD4 `Account` closes its modal through Clerk's `closeSignIn` when it unmounts with the modal open, and stops observing its container first, so that this close never reaches `onSignInClose`.
  - Facts: Clerk holds the modal's state outside the app's routes, and a change of route closes nothing. While open, the modal locks the page's scroll and makes everything outside it inert, and undoes both only when it closes. The screen at `/` and the screen `SignedInOnly` shows stand at different places in the route tree, so Back from the list's address to `/` unmounts the `Account` that opened the form, and its container with it. `openSignIn` and `closeSignIn` chain on one promise in `@clerk/clerk-js` 6.43.1 and take effect in the order called. React's StrictMode runs each effect twice on mount in development. No learner cares whether a form stays open after Back or Forward leaves the address that opened it, by the engineer's judgement.
  - Rejected: The modal's opening and its container held in one provider above the routes, because it needs a context and a way to pass the close to whichever `Account` is mounted, and after Back the close reaches an `Account` that never opened the form, all to keep the form open in a case no learner cares about.
  - Becomes true: a learner who leaves, by Back or Forward, the screen whose form is open finds the next screen usable and unlocked, with no form unless that screen opens its own. A cleanup that closes before it stops observing sends the learner to `/`, and under StrictMode does so at every load of the list's or a deck's address in development; a test unmounts `Account` and asserts that `onSignInClose` is not called.
- CD5 The app loads `@clerk/clerk-js` and `@clerk/ui` from Clerk's CDN at run time, as `@clerk/react` does by default, and rests for the sign-in form only on Clerk's typed API: `openSignIn`, `closeSignIn`, `getContainer`, `oauthFlow` and `useAuth`. It names none of Clerk's markup, class names or internals.
  - Facts: `loadClerkJsScript` and `versionSelector` in `@clerk/shared` load `@clerk/clerk-js` at major version 6 and `@clerk/ui` at major version 1 from Clerk's CDN, and package-lock.json fixes neither, so a change in Clerk's modal reaches learners with no commit and no deploy of the app. CD1, CD2 and CD3 rest on how the modal behaves. `ClerkProvider` takes `ui`, which bundles `@clerk/ui` into the build, and `Clerk`, which bundles `@clerk/clerk-js`. Every test mocks `@clerk/react`.
  - Rejected: `@clerk/ui` bundled through `ui`, because every fix to the sign-in form then waits for the engineer to raise the package, the bundle grows, and `@clerk/clerk-js` still loads from the CDN. Both bundled, because every security fix to signing in then waits for the engineer.
  - Becomes true: fixes to the sign-in form reach learners without a change to the repository. A break in what CD1 and CD2 rest on arrives the same way, with nothing in the repository showing when, and the engineer's check in a browser is the one guard.

## Lesser decisions

- LPD19 The sign-in form shows centred over the screen the learner is on, with that screen dimmed behind it and neither usable nor scrollable until the form closes. A press anywhere on the dimmed screen closes the form. The address stays the same through every step of signing in or of making an account.
  - Facts: under PDR 013, signing in happens in a form over the screen the learner is on, and closing it leaves them there. Under PDR 015, Back on a deck's board returns to the list, and the list's address or a deck's address opened while signed out opens the form. Clerk's form leaves the address unchanged only when it shows centred over a dimmed screen, which it keeps from being used or scrolled and which closes the form when pressed. These facts come from reading Clerk's packages and are not confirmed by a run in a browser.
  - Rejected: the form hung under the "Sign in" button over the undimmed board, with each step of signing in in the address, because each step adds an entry to the browser's history, Back walks through the steps, and after signing in on a deck's address Back stays on the deck once before it reaches the list, against PDR 015; the learner seeing which button opened the form, with the board undimmed, does not outweigh a Back that misleads them. Clerk's centred form moved under "Sign in", because the screen still cannot scroll, the form still closes on a press outside it, and a Clerk update puts the form back in the centre with nothing warning anyone. A form of the app's own under "Sign in", because any step of signing in or of making an account that the app gets wrong or leaves out stops the learner from signing in, and Clerk's form already holds every step.
  - Becomes true: the learner signs in or makes an account without the address changing, and Back and Forward after signing in behave as PDR 015 says. A learner sent by an address meets the form under its heading "Sign in to Sort It", tied to no button, over the dimmed board, which comes back undimmed once they close the form. A stray press on the dimmed screen closes the form and loses what the learner typed in it.
