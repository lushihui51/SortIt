## Product decisions

- PD9 A learner signs in to an account. Every change to their cards is kept as it is made, and signing in on any device brings back the same cards.
  - Facts: the learner edits the cards, for their own sorting. A learner's cards must follow them to any device. PDR 006 ships no accounts and keeps nothing beyond the visit.
  - Rejected: a personal link with no account, because a lost link loses the cards and anyone the link reaches can change them. A file the learner saves and opens again, because nothing follows the learner on its own and changes made on one device are missing on another.
  - Becomes true: the learner finds their cards on every device they sign in on. Every new learner meets a sign-in step.
- PD10 A learner sorts without signing in, and signs in only to create, change or delete cards.
  - Facts: under PD9, signing in is what keeps a learner's cards. A first-time learner has not yet seen what the app does.
  - Rejected: sign-in before anything, because a first-time learner must make an account before seeing what the app does.
  - Becomes true: a first-time learner sorts at once. A learner who sorts without signing in has only the animals deck, and loses their board on a refresh.
- PD25 While signed out, the learner sees "Sign in" at the top of the board. Once signed in, it becomes their profile picture, or the first letter of their email address in a circle when there is no picture, at the top of the board and of the list of decks. Pressing it opens a menu showing the email address they are signed in with and "Sign out".
  - Facts: under PD10, a first-time learner sorts before signing in, and the way in decides whether they learn that decks can be made. A learner may sign in by a way that carries no profile picture.
  - Rejected: "Sign out" and the email address on the list of decks, by the product owner's choice of the profile picture convention. Sign-in and sign-out behind a menu icon, because a first-time learner may never find the way to make decks.
  - Becomes true: the learner sees from the first screen how to sign in, and sees on every screen whose account is in use.
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9
- PD45 A reload on one of a signed-in learner's decks brings them back to that deck's board, with every card in the pool and no dimension chosen.
  - Facts: PDR 006 has a reload start over with an empty board and no dimension chosen, because a board kept across a reload can come back stale. Under PD9, every change to a learner's deck is kept and follows them to any device.
  - Rejected: the list of decks, because the learner loses their place in the app as well as their sort. The board kept as it was, because it can come back stale days later and differs from one device to another while the deck is the same everywhere.
  - Becomes true: a signed-in learner who reloads stays in their deck and keeps every change to it, and starts their sort over.
- PD46 Signing out brings the learner to the board of the app's own animals deck, as a learner who has not signed in meets it, with "Sign in" at the top.
  - Facts: under PD10, a learner who has not signed in sorts the animals deck. Under PD25, "Sign out" sits in the menu behind the learner's picture or letter.
  - Rejected: a screen of its own saying they are signed out, because it adds a screen and a press before the learner can do anything.
  - Becomes true: the learner who signs out meets the app's first screen and sees by "Sign in" that they are out.
- PD47 A change to a learner's deck shows on screen at once, whatever the connection. While one or more changes are not yet kept, a line at the top of the board or the list of decks says so. When the connection returns, the app keeps the waiting changes without reloading the page, the line goes away, and the board stays as the learner left it. A learner who closes the page before then loses the changes that were waiting.
  - Facts: under PD9, every change to a learner's deck is kept as it is made. A learner's connection can drop. Under PD45, a reload starts the sort over.
  - Rejected: undoing the change and saying it could not be kept, because every change made during a drop has to be redone by hand. Locking the deck while the connection is down, because the learner cannot tell whether their last change was kept, and a short drop stops them mid-build. Reloading the page when the connection returns, because it starts over the sort the learner was working on.
  - Becomes true: a learner keeps working through a dropped connection and loses nothing they made, as long as the page stays open, except a change that another device has meanwhile made unfit, which PD59 names.

## Code decisions

- CD1 The app checks who the learner is with Clerk, through `@clerk/react` and its `ClerkProvider`, and takes the learner's id from Clerk's session. The learner signs in through Clerk's ready-made form, opened over the current screen with Google in a popup, and the "Sign in" button, the account button and its menu are the app's own, as in Flashy.
  - Facts: PD49 has the learner sign in with a Google account or an email address and a password, in a form over the screen they are on. Clerk offers both on its free plan, with Google sign-in in a popup. PD25 gives a menu showing only the email address and "Sign out".
  - Rejected: Firebase Authentication and Supabase Auth, by the engineer's choice of Clerk, already proven in Flashy. Auth0, by the same choice. A sign-in form of the app's own, because every sign-in step Clerk can produce becomes the app's to handle.
  - Becomes true: the next developer reuses Flashy's sign-in pattern and its mock of `@clerk/react`. The app needs a domain its owner controls before it can sign anyone in for real.
- CD2 The app is served by GitHub Pages at the root of a domain, or a subdomain, that the engineer owns, through ADR 005's workflow, with Vite's base path set to `/`.
  - Facts: CD1's Clerk needs, for production, a domain whose DNS records its owner can change, and a host's own address such as `lushihui51.github.io` cannot take them. ADR 005 deploys to GitHub Pages under the path `/SortIt/`.
  - Rejected: ADR 005's address at `lushihui51.github.io/SortIt/`, because Clerk cannot run in production there. Netlify, Vercel or Cloudflare Pages, by the engineer's choice of GitHub Pages, which keeps the check and deploy workflow in the repository with no other account.
  - Becomes true: the next developer deploys as ADR 005 says, with the base path `/`, and a rename of the repository no longer breaks every asset. The engineer renews the domain outside the repository, or the app and sign-in go down.
- CD3 The learners' decks are kept in Convex, reached through `ConvexProviderWithClerk` inside `ClerkProvider`. Every Convex function checks the learner by `ctx.auth.getUserIdentity()`, and every deck is owned by the Clerk user id that identity carries. Every change to a deck is a Convex mutation with an optimistic update, and the line PD47 shows stands while Convex reports a mutation in flight.
  - Facts: CD1 checks the learner with Clerk, and CD2 serves the app as static files with no server. Under PD9, every change is kept as it is made. Under PD47, a change shows at once whatever the connection, waits while the connection is down and is kept when it returns without a reload, with a line while any change waits. Convex's client queues and retries mutations through a dropped connection, runs each exactly once, shows an optimistic update while a mutation waits, and reports the mutations in flight.
  - Rejected: Supabase, because the queue PD47 needs would be the app's own to write, and a free project is paused after a week of low use. A server of the app's own, because it needs a host beyond GitHub Pages and the same queue.
  - Becomes true: the next developer writes each change to a deck as one Convex mutation with its optimistic update, and finds the server side in `convex/`. The free plan's hard caps refuse changes once reached.
- CD4 Dragging stays on `@dnd-kit/core` 6, with one pointer sensor for mouse and touch that starts a drag only once the pointer has moved, confined to the components that bind cards, zones and the pool to it. The board keeps no selected card and sends no card: its state holds no selection, and the labels of the pool and the zones are plain text. Each card is a non-interactive element that carries the drag listeners and holds its ⓘ as its one native button, and the ⓘ stops a pointer press from reaching the card's listeners, so pressing it never starts a drag.
  - Facts: PDR 008 moves a card only by dragging, and has a press anywhere on a card but its ⓘ only start a drag. The card is a `<button>` whose click selects it, and the labels of the pool and the zones are buttons that send the selected card. A button inside a button is invalid HTML.
  - Rejected: ADR 002's selecting and sending as app state, because no learner can reach them under PDR 008. The card kept a `<button>` with the ⓘ inside it, because browsers and screen readers treat nested buttons inconsistently, and a press on the ⓘ can fire the card. A pointer sensor with no movement threshold, because every tap on a card would start and end a drag, and a press on the ⓘ that slips by a pixel would drag the card.
  - Becomes true: the next developer finds one way a card moves, the drag, with the `select` action, `selected` and `onPressLabel` gone. The ⓘ is the one focusable part of a card. No component test places a card by a click.
- CD5 Every deck, the app's own and a learner's, takes one shape in `convex/model/deck.ts`: a `Deck` holds an id, a name, its `Dimension`s, each with an id, a name and its `Category`s, each with an id and a name, and its `Card`s, each with an id, a name and `answers`, mapping a dimension's id to one category id of that dimension; a card with no entry for a dimension has no answer in it. Every reference within a deck, and from the board, is by id, and a name is only shown. A learner's card, dimension, category and deck get an id made on the device by `crypto.randomUUID()` when the learner makes it, never changed and never reused. The app's own deck stays ADR 004's bundled JSON file keyed by names and checked by its validator, and is turned into a `Deck` once it passes, each thing's name serving as its id and the deck's id a fixed one. The code calls the collection a deck and each thing sorted a card: src/set/ becomes src/deck/, set.json becomes deck.json with `items` as `cards`, `ItemSet` becomes `Deck`, `Item` becomes `Card`, `shippedSet` becomes `appDeck`, and `SetFault` becomes `DeckFault`, with `noCard`, `duplicateCard` and `card` in place of `noItem`, `duplicateItem` and `item`.
  - Facts: PDR 011 renames cards, dimensions, categories and decks, and a deletion frees a name to be given again. Under PD47 a change shows at once whatever the connection, so a card made offline is placed before any server has seen it. Convex gives a document made by an optimistic update a temporary id and replaces it with the kept one. The board keys every place by an item's name. LPD4 makes "deck" and "card" the words. Convex shares a helper module among its functions and puts shared code in a folder such as `convex/model`, and the app imports any module by a relative path; whether a Convex function imports a module outside `convex/` is not known.
  - Rejected: ADR 004's names everywhere, because a rename rewrites every answer and every place under it, and a category deleted and made again under its old name takes back the cards that lay in the old one. Convex's document ids, because a thing made offline has none until its change is kept, and a place keyed by a temporary id is lost when it is replaced. Ids in the bundled JSON file, because ADR 004's reason holds: a second spelling the author keeps in step by hand. The types in src/deck/, because Convex's functions would import them from outside `convex/`. "Set" and "item" in the code, because one concept takes one word.
  - Becomes true: a rename changes one field. The next developer matches by name only in `findFirstFault` and the name rules. The app imports the deck's types from `convex/model/`.
- CD6 The board's state holds the chosen dimension's id or none, the place of each card by its id, the pool or the zone of a category id, one order of card ids, and whether Check is on, and nothing else. Every action that reads the deck carries the deck as it stands, and the reducer first fits its state to that deck: a card the deck no longer holds leaves the board, a card the board does not hold joins the end of the order in the pool, a card in a zone whose category the chosen dimension no longer holds returns to the pool, and a chosen dimension the deck no longer holds leaves no dimension chosen with every card in the pool. One pure function fits the state the same way at every render. Each card's mark, correct, incorrect or no answer, and the score are computed from the fitted state and the deck wherever they are read, and never stored. Retry returns every card computed incorrect to the pool, permuted among the positions they held. Every action that shuffles carries its source of random numbers, as ADR 003 decided.
  - Facts: under PDR 007 a mark changes the moment its card lands, the no-answer mark shows whether Check is on or off, and the score changes as cards move. Under PDR 011 cards, categories and dimensions are made and deleted while the board shows. The reducer closes over one fixed set and stores the marks set at Check, removing one when its card is selected or its drag starts. React's StrictMode calls a reducer twice on each action.
  - Rejected: ADR 003's marks stored in the state, because they repeat what the places and the answers already say, and an act that forgets them leaves a stale mark with nothing failing. A reducer closing over one deck, because it is made again at every change of the deck, and an action queued under one deck is applied under another. An effect dispatching a change of deck, because the board renders a deleted card at least once, and a case the effect misses leaves it there.
  - Becomes true: the next developer adds an act on a deck without touching the board. Every rule of PDR 007, and every effect of PDR 011 on the board, is a unit test of the reducer or of the fitting function.
- CD7 The board component takes a `Deck` and, for a learner's own deck only, an object of the acts that change it; given none, as for the app's own deck, it shows no field, no ⓘ on a dimension or a zone, and no Save answers. Only the screen of a learner's deck calls Convex, and the board and its parts import nothing of Convex or Clerk.
  - Facts: PDR 010 and PDR 011 put the fields, the ⓘ on dimensions and zones, and Save answers on a learner's own decks alone. Under PD10 a learner who has not signed in sorts the app's own deck. ADR 006 renders components in jsdom.
  - Rejected: a board calling Convex itself behind a flag, because every component test needs a mock of Convex, and the board of a learner who has not signed in sits in a provider it never uses. Two board components, because every rule of sorting is written twice, and the two drift apart with nothing failing.
  - Becomes true: the next developer tests every act on the board and every field in jsdom, with a plain deck and stub acts.
- CD8 The board component renders from a fitted state and a dispatch it is given, and one parent holds the reducer. Component tests build a state by the reducer's own actions, cards placed included, render the board from it, and assert what shows and what is dispatched. Dragging is checked by hand in the served browsers at each cycle's review.
  - Facts: under CD4 a card is placed only through dnd-kit's pointer sensor. ADR 006 records that jsdom gives no element a position or a size, so no drag runs there.
  - Rejected: a browser automation tool, because it adds browser downloads and a second runner, and with Clerk and Convex it needs signed-in test accounts. A prop for an initial state used only by tests, because a component of the app carries a prop only tests use. Firing dnd-kit's drag end by hand, because the tests then rest on dnd-kit's internals.
  - Becomes true: every rule of PDR 007 shown on screen has a component test. A broken drop handler fails only in the check by hand.
- CD9 Each deck of a learner is one document in the Convex table `decks`, holding the deck's own id, its owner's Clerk user id, by which the table is indexed, its name, its dimensions with their categories, and its cards with an `answers` record keyed by dimension id, in CD5's shape. Each act on a deck is one mutation taking the deck's id and the act's arguments, never the whole deck, which reads the deck, applies the act and writes the deck back.
  - Facts: a Convex mutation runs serializably and commits all its writes or none. A Convex document holds at most 1 MiB, 16 levels of nesting and 8192 elements per array, and a record's key is any non-empty ASCII string not starting with "$" or "_". Under CD3 every change is a mutation with an optimistic update. PDR 001 names a deck of about 30 cards and 6 zones. PDR 009 holds names within one deck, and deck names across one learner's decks.
  - Rejected: a table each for decks, dimensions, categories, cards and answers, because a board joins five tables, deleting a dimension cascades across three, and an optimistic update edits several query results in step, so a missed one leaves a stray row with nothing failing. Mutations replacing the whole deck, because one device's change overwrites another's, and a change queued offline replays a stale deck. A deck's name in a document apart from its contents, because every rename and every new deck then writes two documents, to save reading the decks whole on the list.
  - Becomes true: the next developer finds a whole deck in one document, in the app's own shape. Two devices' acts never overwrite each other. A deck holds at most 8192 cards and 1 MiB in all, and a change past either is refused by the mutation and not kept.
- CD10 Each act on a learner's deck is one pure function in `convex/model/deckActs.ts`, taking a `Deck` and the act's arguments and returning the changed deck or the refusal PDR 009 names. The app applies it before sending the mutation and shows a refusal without sending, applies it again as the mutation's optimistic update, and the mutation applies it to the kept deck.
  - Facts: PDR 009 refuses a name as the learner types, keeping what they typed and changing nothing. Under PD47 a change shows at once whatever the connection. Under CD3 every change is a mutation with an optimistic update, and under CD9 each mutation reads the deck, applies the act and writes the deck back. Convex reruns an optimistic update when the query results it changes are updated.
  - Rejected: the name rules checked only in the mutation, because a learner without a connection sees no refusal until it returns, and their typed name is gone by then. An optimistic update written apart from the mutation, because the two drift, and the screen shows a change other than the one kept until the server's result replaces it.
  - Becomes true: the next developer writes an act once and unit-tests its rules under Vitest. What shows at once is what is kept, as long as the kept deck has not changed meanwhile.
- CD11 The Convex table `learners` holds one row, keyed by Clerk user id, for each learner given the copy of the app's own deck. When a signed-in learner's app starts, it builds a copy of `appDeck` with ids made on the device and calls the mutation `startLearner`, which adds the learner's row and the copy in one transaction when the learner has no row, and does nothing otherwise.
  - Facts: PDR 012 starts every learner's list with a copy of the app's own deck, and a learner who deletes the copy cannot get it back. A Convex mutation runs serializably and commits all its writes or none. How a Convex function reads the bundled JSON file or makes a random id is not known.
  - Rejected: the copy given whenever a learner has no deck, because a learner who deletes every deck gets it back. The copy built in Convex, because it rests on the two unknowns. A Clerk webhook on a new account, because it needs an HTTP endpoint and a signing secret, and the list can show empty before the webhook arrives.
  - Becomes true: two devices signing in for the first time at once make one copy. The next developer reads whether a learner has started from one table, never from their decks.
- CD12 ADR 005's workflow keeps its two jobs. The check job's typecheck covers `convex/`, and the deploy job runs `npx convex deploy --cmd 'npm run build'` with `CONVEX_DEPLOY_KEY` from a repository secret and the Clerk publishable key from a repository variable, then deploys dist/ to Pages. Development runs `npx convex dev` beside the dev server.
  - Facts: `CONVEX_DEPLOY_KEY` names the deployment in CI. `npx convex deploy --cmd` runs the build with `VITE_CONVEX_URL` set to the production deployment, then pushes the functions. Under ADR 005 the check job builds, and the deploy job runs only after it passes.
  - Rejected: Convex deployed by hand, because the page and the functions live then drift apart with nothing failing. A second workflow for Convex, for the reason ADR 005 rejects one.
  - Becomes true: the functions go live a moment before the page, and a page left open keeps calling them for longer, so the next developer keeps every function accepting the previous page's calls. The engineer adds one secret and one variable to the repository. AGENTS.md's Commands gain the Convex development command.
- CD13 Every Convex function that reads or changes a deck loads it through one helper, which finds the deck by its id and refuses unless the Clerk user id `ctx.auth.getUserIdentity()` gives is the deck's owner.
  - Facts: under CD3 every Convex function checks the learner by `ctx.auth.getUserIdentity()`, and every deck is owned by a Clerk user id.
  - Rejected: the owner check written in each function, because one function that misses it lets any signed-in learner change another's deck with nothing failing.
  - Becomes true: the next developer adds a function on a deck by calling the helper. A function that skips the helper is the one place the check fails silently.

  - Rejected: a longer two-line question, because it is hard to read. Naming the dimensions in the question before deleting a deck, because the question gets longer to read.

  - Facts: the product owner looked for how to make a dimension on an empty deck and did not find the dashed field. A learner building a deck types many names in a row.
  - Rejected: buttons reading "+ New card" and so on that turn into a field when pressed, by the product owner's choice of fields typed into at once. Fields with a "+" inside and a solid outline, by the same choice.
