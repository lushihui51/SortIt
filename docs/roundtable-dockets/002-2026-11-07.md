## Lesser decisions

- LPD1 While Check is on and no card lies in a zone, no score shows.
  - Facts: under PDR 007, Check stays on or off across Reset and a change of dimension, so Check can be on with an empty board.
  - Rejected: "0 of 0 correct", because it reads as a failure before the learner has placed anything.
  - Becomes true: the learner meets a score only once a card lies in a zone.
  - Drawings: 2, 5
- LPD2 Check is a switch labelled "Check" whose position shows whether it is on or off.
  - Facts: under PDR 007, Check has two states the learner switches between. Some learners cannot tell colours apart (PDR 007).
  - Rejected: a button that stays pressed in while on, because the pressed look is easy to miss, especially on a phone. A button whose label changes between "Show marks" and "Hide marks", because one control carries two labels and the label can be misread as the state.
  - Becomes true: the learner reads at a glance, without colour, whether verdicts are showing.
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9
- LPD3 The app shows no hint beside Check.
  - Facts: under PDR 007, Check is never unavailable.
  - Rejected: a line explaining what Check does, because one press already shows it and the line would stay on screen permanently.
  - Becomes true: the learner sees the Check switch with nothing beside it.
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9

- LPD4 The learner calls a collection of cards with its dimensions a "deck", and each thing they sort a "card". The words "set" and "item" appear nowhere the learner reads.
  - Facts: under PDR 012, the learner keeps and names several collections. A collection's items already appear as cards. "Board" names the sorting area in every record.
  - Rejected: "board" for the collection, because it already names the sorting area and suggests one sorting session rather than a collection kept. "Set" and "item", by the product owner's choice of words.
  - Becomes true: the learner reads "deck" and "card" on every screen, the error PDR 009 shows included. The vocabulary changes to the new words, and the records keep theirs.
- LPD5 The learner reads "dimension" for one way a deck can be sorted, such as Habitat, and "category" for one of its groups, such as Ocean, wherever the app names them.
  - Facts: under PDR 011, the learner creates and changes decks, so the screen names the ways to sort and their groups. Every record already uses "dimension" and "category" in these meanings.
  - Rejected: "way to sort", because it is three words. "Property", "trait" and "sort", by the product owner's choice of words.
  - Becomes true: the learner and every record use one word for each concept.
- LPD6 The button that saves the board as the answers reads "Save answers".
  - Facts: the learner already reads "answer" for a card's category in the error PDR 009 shows. A learner may press a vaguely labelled button thinking it keeps their progress.
  - Rejected: "Set answers", because it does not say that the answers are kept. "Save", because it does not say what is kept. "Lock in", because it sounds final and reads like submitting a quiz attempt.
  - Becomes true: the learner reads on the button exactly what it changes.
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 9
- LPD7 The name refusals read: for a deck name the learner already uses, "You already have a deck called "<Deck>"."; for a deck with an empty name, "A deck needs a name."; for a card name the deck already has, new or renamed, "The deck "<Deck>" already has a card called "<Card>"."; for renaming a card to an empty name, "A card needs a name." Each shows as a line under the field, which keeps what the learner typed.
  - Facts: PDR 009 and PDR 012 refuse these names. Under PDR 009, two decks may each hold a card of one name, so a refusal names what holds the name. The product owner wants every message to name the kind of thing and its name.
  - Rejected: "That name is already taken.", because it does not say what holds the name. Adding "Choose another name.", because the field keeping the typed text already says so.
  - Becomes true: the learner reads in each refusal what holds the name, by kind and by name.
- LPD8 Before "Save answers" changes existing answers, the learner reads "Change the answers of <N> cards for the dimension "<Dimension>"?" ("1 card" for one), with "Save answers" and "Cancel". Before a card is deleted, the learner reads "Delete the card "<Card>"? Its answers will be lost.", with "Delete" and "Cancel".
  - Facts: PDR 010 and PDR 011 ask the learner before these acts. A learner who confirms out of habit learns what is at stake only from the question's words. The product owner wants every message to name the kind of thing and its name.
  - Rejected: "Are you sure?", because it does not say what is at stake. "OK" as the confirming button, because it does not say what happens.
  - Becomes true: the learner reads in each question what changes, and agrees by pressing the act's own word.
- LPD9 Before a category is deleted, the learner reads "Delete the category "<Category>"? <N> cards' answers will be lost." ("1 card's answer" for one; the question alone when no card has it). Before a dimension is deleted, the learner reads "Delete the dimension "<Dimension>"? <N> categories and their answers will be lost." ("1 category" for one). Before a deck is deleted, the learner reads "Delete the deck "<Deck>"? <N> cards and their answers will be lost." ("1 card" for one; the question alone for a deck with no card). Each question offers "Delete" and "Cancel".
  - Facts: under PDR 011, every deletion asks first and names what is lost. Under LPD8, the question before deleting a card names what is lost. The product owner wants each message to name the kind of thing, its name, and what is lost, in one short line.
  - Rejected: a longer two-line question, because it is hard to read. Naming the dimensions in the question before deleting a deck, because the question gets longer to read.
  - Becomes true: the learner reads before each deletion how much is lost, in one short line.
- LPD10 A deck with no dimension shows "Add cards and a dimension to begin" where the zones will be, in place of "Choose how to sort to begin".
  - Facts: under PDR 011, a new deck opens on its board with no card and no dimension. The learner makes cards before dimensions, by the product owner's order of steps. "Choose how to sort to begin" asks the learner to choose from nothing on an empty deck.
  - Rejected: "Choose how to sort to begin" on an empty deck, because there is nothing to choose. A separate first step asking for the first dimension and its categories, because it makes dimensions a second way, unlike the board's.
  - Becomes true: the learner opening an empty deck reads what to make first, beside the fields that make it.
  - Drawings: 5
- LPD11 The "New card", "New dimension", "New category" and "New deck" fields show as dashed outlines holding their name in grey, and the learner types straight into them. While one has the learner's focus, it shows a solid blue outline.
  - Facts: the product owner looked for how to make a dimension on an empty deck and did not find the dashed field. A learner building a deck types many names in a row.
  - Rejected: buttons reading "+ New card" and so on that turn into a field when pressed, by the product owner's choice of fields typed into at once. Fields with a "+" inside and a solid outline, by the same choice.
  - Becomes true: the learner types a new name with no press first, and sees clearly which field they are typing in.
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9
- LPD12 While signed out, the learner sees "Sign in" at the top of the board. Once signed in, it becomes their profile picture, or the first letter of their email address in a circle when there is no picture, at the top of the board and of the list of decks. Pressing it opens a menu showing the email address they are signed in with and "Sign out".
  - Facts: under PDR 013, a first-time learner sorts before signing in, and the way in decides whether they learn that decks can be made. A learner may sign in by a way that carries no profile picture.
  - Rejected: "Sign out" and the email address on the list of decks, by the product owner's choice of the profile picture convention. Sign-in and sign-out behind a menu icon, because a first-time learner may never find the way to make decks.
  - Becomes true: the learner sees from the first screen how to sign in, and sees on every screen whose account is in use.
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9
- LPD13 Signing out brings the learner to the board of the app's own deck, as a learner who has not signed in meets it, with "Sign in" at the top.
  - Facts: under PDR 013, a learner who has not signed in sorts the app's own deck. Under LPD12, "Sign out" sits in the menu behind the learner's picture or letter.
  - Rejected: a screen of its own saying they are signed out, because it adds a screen and a press before the learner can do anything.
  - Becomes true: the learner who signs out meets the app's first screen and sees by "Sign in" that they are out.
- LPD14 While changes are not yet kept, the line PDR 014 shows reads "Your changes are not saved yet. Keep this page open."
  - Facts: under PDR 014, a learner who closes the page while changes wait loses them. The trouble can lie with the learner's connection or with the app.
  - Rejected: "No connection. Your changes will be saved when it returns.", because it is wrong when the learner's connection is fine and does not warn against closing the page. "Saving…", because it reads as routine and the learner closes the page.
  - Becomes true: the learner reads in the line the one thing that would lose their work.
- LPD15 The sign-in form shows Clerk's own words throughout, for signing in, for making an account, and for every step and message along the way, and "Secured by Clerk" shows under it.
  - Facts: under PDR 013, the learner signs in, or makes their account, in a form over the screen they are on, with Google or with an email address and a password. Clerk's form comes with its own words for every line and every step, errors and email code checks included. On Clerk's free plan, the form always carries a "Secured by" line with Clerk's logo, and removing it takes a paid plan with a monthly cost.
  - Rejected: the app's own words for every line, because any line not replaced shows in Clerk's words, and one form speaks in two voices. The app's own heading and greeting with Clerk's words elsewhere, by the product owner's choice of Clerk's words throughout. Removing "Secured by Clerk" on a paid plan, because the app pays monthly for one line, and the learner gains nothing else.
  - Becomes true: the learner meets sign-in wording that reads as it does on many sites, and sees who handles their sign-in. A first-time learner reads "Welcome back!". A learner who has never heard of Clerk meets a name that is not Sort It.
  - Drawings: 7
- LPD16 The sign-in form hangs under the "Sign in" button, over the right side of the board, which stays undimmed. It opens the same way when the learner presses "Sign in" and when a deck's address or the list's address opens it.
  - Facts: under PDR 013, signing in happens in a form over the screen the learner is on, and closing it leaves them there. Under LPD12, "Sign in" sits at the top of the board while signed out. Under PDR 015, the list's address or a deck's address opened while signed out opens the form.
  - Rejected: centred over a dimmed board, as Clerk shows it by default, because a learner sent by an address sees nothing of what opened the form, and the dimming hides the board they are kept on.
  - Becomes true: the learner sees the form come out of "Sign in", with their board in plain view around it. The form covers the cards at the right of the pool while it is open.
  - Drawings: 7
- LPD17 The line PDR 015 shows when a deck's address names a deck the learner cannot open reads "This deck cannot be opened. It may have been deleted, or belong to another account." It stays until the learner leaves the list of decks, by opening a deck or by reloading.
  - Facts: under PDR 015, nothing of another learner's deck shows, so the line may not name the deck, and the line explains why the learner landed on the list instead of the deck. The product owner wants every message to name the kind of thing. A deck cannot be opened because it was deleted or because it belongs to another learner.
  - Rejected: "This deck is not in your decks.", because a learner who remembers owning the deck may think the app lost it. "This deck cannot be opened.", because without a reason the learner cannot tell whether to try again. A × that removes it, because it adds a button for a line that goes away on its own. Fading after a few seconds, because a learner who looks away or reads slowly misses why they are there.
  - Becomes true: the learner recognises which of the two causes applies to them, reads the line at their own pace, and does not see it when they come back to the list.
- LPD18 The line PDR 014 shows for a change not kept holds one line per change not kept, in two sentences: what was not kept, "Your new <kind> "<Name>" was not kept.", "Renaming the <kind> "<Name>" to "<New name>" was not kept." or "Your change to the <kind> "<Name>" was not kept."; then why, the refusal LPD7 and VC25 to VC28 give for a name taken, or "It was deleted on another device." or "The <kind> "<Name>" was deleted on another device." for something deleted. A Save answers kept in part under PDR 014 gives one line for each card or category it leaves out: "Your answer for the card "<Card>" was not kept." or "Your answers in the category "<Category>" were not kept.", then "It was deleted on another device." A later change to a thing whose making was not kept gives no line of its own, and the line for its making opens "Your new <kind> "<Name>" and your later changes to it were not kept." Each line stays until the learner leaves the screen it shows on, by opening a deck, going back to the list of decks, or reloading.
  - Facts: under PDR 014, a line names what was not kept and why, so the learner can redo it. Under LPD7, every refusal names the kind of thing and its name. The product owner wants every message to name the kind of thing and its name. Under LPD17, the line for a deck that cannot be opened stays until the learner leaves the list. Under PDR 014, a Save answers kept late keeps the answers of the cards and categories the deck still holds. A thing whose making was not kept is not on the screen, so a later change to it finds nothing to change.
  - Rejected: one line for all, "Some changes you made while offline were not kept.", because the learner cannot tell which changes to redo. Each line without its reason, because the learner cannot tell whether to retry under another name. A × on each line, because it adds a button the line LPD17 gives does not have. Fading after a few seconds, because a learner busy dragging, or reading slowly, misses which change to redo. One line per card for a deleted category, because a category of many cards fills the top of the board with lines saying one thing. "Your change to the card "<Card>" was not kept." for an answer left out, because the learner pressed Save answers and does not read which act was lost. A line for each later change to a thing whose making was not kept, with "Its making was not kept." as why, because one lost thing makes several lines naming one cause.
  - Becomes true: the learner reads which change to redo, and why, in the words of the refusals they already know, at their own pace, and both kinds of line go away the same way. Several lines take room from the board until the learner leaves it. The learner reads one line for each thing lost. A learner who renamed a thing after making it reads it under the name they first gave it.
  - Drawings: 9

- LCD1 The code calls the collection a deck and each thing sorted a card: src/set/ becomes src/deck/, set.json becomes deck.json, `ItemSet` becomes `Deck`, `Item` becomes `Card`, `shippedSet` becomes `appDeck`, and `SetFault` becomes `DeckFault`, with `noCard`, `duplicateCard` and `card` in place of `noItem`, `duplicateItem` and `item`.
  - Facts: LPD4 makes "deck" and "card" the learner's words. ADR 007 gives every deck the type `Deck` in `convex/model/deck.ts`, and keeps the app's own deck in one JSON file whose cards lie under `cards`.
  - Rejected: "set" and "item" in the code, because one concept takes one word.
  - Becomes true: the next developer reads in the code the words the learner reads.
